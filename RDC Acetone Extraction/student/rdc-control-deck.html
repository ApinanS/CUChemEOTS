How the loops were chosen

Five pairing rules

  1. Direct, fast, one signthe step tests give gain and time
  2. Fast loops on the flowsreject pump disturbances before the column sees them
  3. Inventory on its own outletlight phase: pressure; heavy phase: interface
  4. Fail to the safe statefeed, solvent, LV-109 close; PV-108 opens
  5. Solvent follows feeda lost feed never leaves solvent flowing alone

$K,\ \tau,\ \theta$
process gain, time constant, dead time

$\lambda$

$K=\dfrac{\Delta PV\%}{\Delta OUT\%},\ \ \tau=1.5(t_{63}-t_{28}),\ \ \theta=t_{63}-\tau$two-point fit of a first-order-plus-dead-time curve

Part 3 · summary

What part 3 settled

  • A 5 % step in MAN gives K, τ, θ; lambda gives Kc and Ti.
  • The temperature loops can be four times faster than as built.
  • Keep the interface loop slow: the settler is the buffer.

This deck

Key Takeaways

  • Rank objectives and count degrees of freedom before drawing loops.
  • Pair each CV with a direct, fast handle of one sign.
  • Put each inventory on its own outlet; fail valves to the safe state.
  • Tie the solvent to the feed by ratio; leave quality to the operator.
  • Tune from a step test, then check on a copy of the model.
