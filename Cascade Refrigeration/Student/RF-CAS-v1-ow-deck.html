One number per limit

Margin: distance to the limit in scale unitsstep 1 of 2

Every bound is judged the same way.

$x_j$
value of the variable of bound $j$
its unit
$x_{lo,j},\,x_{hi,j}$
low and high limit (alarm, trip, valve range)
its unit
$s_j$
scale of that variable, set by the tab (e.g. 1 bar, 0.5 MW, 5 t/h)
its unit
$m_j,\ M$
margin of bound $j$; margin of the point
–
$$m_j=\min\!\left(\frac{x_j-x_{lo,j}}{s_j},\ \frac{x_{hi,j}-x_j}{s_j}\right)\qquad M=\min_j m_j$$

Inside the window when every bound holds: $M \ge 0$.

Part 1 · summary

What part 1 settled

  • Variability, bounds, capacity and flexibility define the window.
  • Twelve handles hold twelve variables; disturbance ranges are assumed.
  • Each of 47 bounds gets a margin $m_j$; the point is inside when $M \ge 0$.
  • At design $M = 0.97$, nearest the feed alarm band.

Read the window with care

What the maps do not say

  • Steady state only: nothing about how fast it moves.
  • Trips moved away in the maps; the real plant stops.
  • Bottom at 20 t/h is an alarm; the model chills down to 5 t/h.
  • Assumed: disturbance ranges, some critical limits.
  • Grid steps 2–4 t/h, 2 K: edges are interpolated.

Part 3 · summary

What part 3 settled

  • Every outside preset fails on a K-201 limit.
  • Warm water or high feed: PI-206, PSHH-206 or driver alarm first.
  • With trips live, PSHH-206 stops K-201, then K-301.
  • 87.0 % of drawn days are inside; driver power limits most.

Key Takeaways

1

Judge every operating point against all bounds, root cause first.

2

Read the margin, not just inside or outside: it shows which limit is next.

3

Watch K-201 power and discharge pressure on hot afternoons and at high feed.

4

Use the minutes between alarm and trip, and know which limit comes next.
