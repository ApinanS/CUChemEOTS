1 · Phases and revisions

Five phases, one document trail

In an EPC contract one contractor designs, buys, builds and hands over. Documents are born in FEED and detailed engineering; the later phases consume them.

1 · Phases and revisions

A status says how far you may trust it

Each issue of a document carries a status. The status, not the date, tells the reader what may be done with it.

4 · Rules of credit

One percentage from many documents step 1 of 3

A manager asks "how far is engineering?" Each document earns part of its weight at each issue.

$$P = \frac{\sum_i w_i\,c_i}{\sum_i w_i}$$
$P$
engineering progress
%
$w_i$
weight of document $i$: its PDF pages (tab-only: 2)
pages
$c_i$
credit of its present status
%

Credits and page weights assumed; real projects weight by budgeted man-hours.

4 · Rules of credit · worked example

Progress of this set step 2 of 3

From the previous slide$P = \sum_i w_i c_i \,/\, \sum_i w_i$

Assumed status: PFD, both P&IDs and the HMB at IFC; lists and studies at IFA; the rest at IFR.

$\sum w_i = 19 + 108 + 45 = 172$pages at IFC, IFA, IFR
$\sum w_i c_i = 19(1.00) + 108(0.70) + 45(0.40) = 112.6$credit 100, 70, 40 %
$P = 112.6 / 172 = 65.5\ \%$weighted progress

The HAZOP report alone is 81 of 172 pages: page weight flatters long reports.

Key Takeaways

1

Read a document's status before you use its numbers.

2

Number every document by the scheme so it explains itself.

3

Cite each input by number and revision, or revisions get lost.

4

Write every inconsistency as a query; never fix it silently.
