Part 2 · summary

What part 2 settled

  • Capacity ÷ peak release: 1.36 at 32 °C CW, 1.03 at 38 °C.
  • Most of the margin is E-101; the jacket is UA-limited.
  • Valve position is not spare capacity.
  • The recipe range is the overlap: about 1.5 : 1.

$g_{ij}$: gain of output i (T, TI-110) to handle j (TV-110B, FV-130).

E-101 water moves 16 × more heat than the jacket valve; λ₁₁ ≈ 0, so VPC-130 pairs by time scale.

Holes, and what the window does not tell you

Read the window with its assumptions

  • No holes: no failed batch inside the feasible region.
  • A batch is one point; short upsets are a control question.
  • Every batch starts charged at 57 °C; dose and activity lumped.
  • Model mismatch (±10 %, assumed) not swept: stay off the edge.

Part 3 · summary

What part 3 settled

  • Type I: frozen handles hold only briefly; feedback is required.
  • Type III: 27 of 112 clean batches inside; fouling shrinks it.
  • Monte Carlo: about 90 % of typical batches inside.
  • E-101 is the strong handle; VPC-130 uses it slowly.

Key Takeaways

1

Judge a batch by its worst value anywhere between initiator and end point.

2

List the variability first, then size capacity for its worst case.

3

Check capacity against the peak release at the warmest cooling water.

4

Read valve position as travel left, never as duty left.

5

Keep the design batch away from the window's edge by the model's uncertainty.
