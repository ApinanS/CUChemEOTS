What if the plan changes?

The rules are typical. Move one item and recheck every rule at once.

C-201 can move 13 m east and the rack can narrow to 6 m, but the control room tolerates only 20 m closer before the 60 m rule fails.

Part 1 · summary

What part 1 settled

  • Sizes come from the documents; positions are a design choice.
  • Spacing rules protect against fire, blast and ignition.
  • The control room sits upwind, off-plot, beyond 60 m.
  • One move can break several rules: recheck them all.

Where does liquid collect?

Flare lines fall all the way; the C-201 suction line's low point is the machine itself (EQ-ly-02).

Part 2 · summary

What part 2 settled

  • Platforms sit 0.5–2.0 m below the nozzles they serve.
  • R-201 height trades dump clearance against stair length.
  • V-201 height is set by the fire case, not by LV-204.
  • Relief lines must free-drain; a suction low point needs a pot.

Part 3 · summary

What part 3 settled

  • Isometrics give every fitting, elevation and support of one line.
  • The take-off counts welds, flanges and supports by rule.
  • Every platform needs two ways down or a short dead end.
  • Emergency valves belong at grade or on a stair-served floor.

Key Takeaways

1

Take sizes from the documents; label every position and spacing as assumed.

2

Recheck every spacing rule after moving any item.

3

Set platforms by the nozzles and vessels by drains and relief cases.

4

Slope suction and relief lines so liquid never reaches a machine.

5

Give every platform two ways down and every emergency valve stair access.
