Lessons from the runs

Why transitions are hard

A path, not a point

Modes go MAN → AUTO in order; set points are ramped.

The process changes

Bed 1 inlet dips to °C when steam is cut.

Protection changes

FSLL-201 is armed only while XV-202 is open.

Controllers saturate

PIC-203 at 0 % while filling: pressure peaks bar g.

Part 1 · summary

What part 1 settled

  • Circulation first, heat second, reactants third, rate last.
  • Seven permissives on XV-202 enforce that order.
  • Half-open E-204 steam never reaches 200 °C.
  • A moderate ramp is the fastest start without a bed excursion.

Start-up added flow, heat and reactants in that order. Shutdown removes them in reverse.

Regeneration

Catalyst change: one converter, so a planned shutdown

Hot standby keeps the catalyst. Every few years the catalyst itself must go.

Why

Cu crystallites sinter and poisons build up: activity falls.

How

Cool on N₂, depressure, dump, reload, reduce in place.

Hazard

Reduced copper is pyrophoric: passivate or dump under N₂.

A crude-methanol tank keeps distillation running during part of the outage (Part 3).

Part 2 · summary

What part 2 settled

Does the ramp rate matter?

The peak barely moves with ramp rate; the destination sets the margin. A slow ramp costs time, and gives the operator time to see the limit coming.

Feed-composition change · the heat of reaction

Why CO-rich make-up heats the beds

Part 3 · summary

What part 3 settled

  • The front end sets the rate; the beds set the ramp.
  • The destination, not the ramp, decides the bed margin.
  • CO-rich gas heats bed 1; act on the warning.
  • Raise inlets only as the catalyst ages.

Key Takeaways

1

Add flow, heat, reactants, then rate; remove them in reverse.

2

Treat permissives as the minimum; check the whole converter before admitting gas.

3

Ramp the make-up moderately and watch both bed peak and pressure.

4

Judge a rate change by where it ends, not how fast it goes.

5

Move bed inlets ahead of a known feed change, and only as the catalyst ages.
