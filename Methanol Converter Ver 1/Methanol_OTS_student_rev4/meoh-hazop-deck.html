IEC 61882:2016

Four phases, one of them the meeting

1

Definition

Scope: make-up boundary round the loop to the let-down drum, purge and flare.

2

Preparation

P&ID, balances, alarm list, procedures; nodes and intents.

3

Examination

Node by node, guide word by guide word, recorded live.

4

Documentation

Worksheets, register, owners, close-out, revalidation.

Phase 3 · examination

The loop the team repeats for every node

Every meaningful deviation is written down, even when its safeguards are adequate (“full recording”), so the reasoning can be audited later.

Boundaries sit where the intent changes, so each node can be judged against one sentence of numbers.

Interactive · pick a guide word

Eleven guide words generate the deviations

Part 1 · summary

What part 1 settled

  • Each node is compared with its numbered design intent.
  • IEC 61882: four phases; the meeting is the third.
  • Nine nodes: eight equipment sections plus procedures.
  • Guide word × parameter gave 74 deviations, all eleven words used.

Node N5 · converter R-201

Cold shots hold each bed inlet

Intent: four adiabatic beds, inlets 220 / 225 / 230 / 235 °C, exits ≈ 260 / 277 / 270 / 268 °C.

Each TIC (temperature indicating controller) opens its quench valve to cool the next bed inlet.

Quench valves TV-221/231/241 fail open: on air loss, more cold gas.

Interactive · step through the row

N5 · Flow + NO = “no quench to bed 2”

Consequences are written as if every safeguard failed; only then are the safeguards listed and the risk ranked.

Interactive · drag S and L

Rank the row: risk = S × L

The worked row is 4×2=84\times2=84×2=8, Medium: review safeguards. Of 74 rows, 4 are High, 58 Medium, 12 Low, none Very high.

Interactive · fail one element

Which safeguards survive a single failure?

Alarm and trip share one thermocouple and one valve, so one failure can remove both: hence R04 (2oo3 elements) and R05 (XV-202B).

TIAH = temperature alarm high · TSHH = temperature switch high-high (trip I-1) · 2oo3 = two-out-of-three voting.

Interactive · model run, TV-221 closed

How hot does bed 2 really get?

The worksheet says “approaches the trip”. The model can say how close.

— TI-224 bed 2 exit · — TIC-221 bed 2 inlet · — TI-234 bed 3 exit

Bed 2 exit levels out below the 300 °C trip: the alarm works, but no SIS function ends the excursion.

Part 2 · summary

What part 2 settled

  • A row runs deviation → causes → unprotected consequence → safeguards → risk → action.
  • “No quench to bed 2” ranks S4 × L2 = 8, Medium.
  • Its alarm and trip share elements and XV-202: R04, R05, R26.
  • The model holds bed 2 below the trip: the alarm is the working layer.

Dynamic check · set-up

Each deviation run on a copy of the model

A conventional HAZOP judges consequences qualitatively. The simulator can time them.

Start

Design point: loop 75 bar g, make-up 365.6 kNm³/h.

Safeguards on

Alarms, SIS trips with 5 s delay, controllers in normal mode.

Nobody acts

No operator action; 30 min, or 5 min past a trip.

Interactive · pick a deviation

Ten deviations, first alarm and trip

Only 3 of 10 deviations reach a trip; the other seven rely on alarms, fail positions or nothing at all.

Interactive · operator response time

Does the alarm leave enough time?

LOPA credits an alarm only with ≥ 10 min to act.

From first alarm to trip or empty boot the model gives under 3 minutes: these alarms are warnings, not protection layers.

Dynamic check · findings

Three things the worksheet alone would miss

Alarm points the wrong way

PT-203 low: PIC-203 shows LO while true pressure reaches 83.0 bar g. → R08

No trip on low level

LV-204 open: boot empty at 05:31, nothing trips. → R02, R03

Supply caps pressure

Purge blocked: PSHH-203 trips at 02:54, peak 85.05 bar g, below PSV-201 at 88.

Interactive · credit the layers · LOPA tab, scenario L1

Is the bed runaway rare enough?

Installed layers miss the 10⁻³ /yr target about twelvefold; the 2oo3 trip with XV-202B (R04, R05) meets it.

IPL = independent protection layer · PFD = probability of failure on demand · RRF = mitigated frequency ÷ target · BPCS = basic process control system.

Interactive · the register, by node

29 recommendations, each with an owner

Report MEOH-V1-SA-HZP-001: 59 pages, a close-out sheet per action. MOC = management of change; revalidate about every five years.

Procedures (N9) carry the largest share of actions: start-up and shutdown create their own hazards.

Part 3 · summary

What part 3 settled

  • Ten model runs: three trip, seven rely on alarms or nothing.
  • Alarm to consequence takes minutes: no alarm credit.
  • The runs exposed a misleading PT-203 alarm and no low-level trip.
  • Scenario L1 needs the 2oo3 trip and XV-202B.

Key Takeaways

1

Write every node's design intent with numbers before using a guide word.

2

Judge consequences as if every safeguard failed; then credit only independent ones.

3

Check that alarm, trip and valve do not share one element.

4

Time the worst deviations on the simulator before trusting an alarm.

5

Send trip-protected scenarios to LOPA to set their SIL.
